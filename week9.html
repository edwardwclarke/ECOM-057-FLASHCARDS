Week 9 – Growth Investing Flashcards

What is a growth investor?
Someone who estimates the value of growth assets better than the market.
What are the four types of growth investors?
Small Cap, IPO, Passive Screener, Activist Investor
Why can small caps outperform?
They have more growth potential, especially during high inflation or downward yield curves.
What are risks of investing in small caps?
Higher transaction costs, less analyst coverage, and high volatility.
What is IPO underpricing?
Initial shares are often priced 10–15% below market value to attract investors.
What's the allocation problem in IPO investing?
You often get fewer shares in good IPOs and more in less popular ones.
What does a passive screener do?
Ranks stocks based on indicators like earnings growth and PEG ratios.
Why is PEG ratio useful?
It helps compare stock prices relative to their earnings growth. Lower PEG = cheaper growth.
What does an activist growth investor do?
Takes an active role in management to unlock value and guide company growth.
