Week 9: Growth Investing – Flashcards

What is a growth investor?
Someone who tries to estimate the value of future growth assets better than the market.
Name the four types of growth investors.
Small Cap Investor, IPO Investor, Passive Screener, Activist Investor
What is the small cap premium?
The idea that small firms earn higher returns than large firms of similar risk, but it's cyclical and volatile.
Why do small caps carry higher transaction costs?
They have wider bid-ask spreads and greater price impact when traded, especially in the short term.
What is IPO underpricing?
It’s when the initial share price is set below market value, often by 10–15%, creating a first-day jump in price.
What is the allocation problem in IPO investing?
You may get more shares in lower-quality IPOs and fewer in high-demand ones, reducing expected gains.
What is the goal of a passive screener?
To use indicators like earnings momentum or PEG ratios to find undervalued growth stocks.
Why is earnings growth a risky indicator?
It's volatile, mean-reverting, and affected by non-operational factors like taxes and leverage.
What does a low PEG ratio suggest?
That a stock is cheap relative to its earnings growth; may generate higher average returns before adjusting for risk.
What is the strategy of an activist growth investor?
To intervene in company management to unlock hidden value and growth potential.
