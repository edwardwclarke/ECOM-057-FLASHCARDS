📚 Value Investing - Week 8 Flashcards

Introduction
What is a value investor?

Someone who buys a stock if its price is lower than the value of its current assets. Any future growth is a bonus.

Like buying a shop worth £70 for just £50.
Introduction
Three types of value investors

Passive Screener, Contrarian, and Activist investors each use a different method to find undervalued stocks.

One uses a checklist, one buys unpopular things, one tries to fix broken things.
Activist
Who are activist investors?

Individuals, institutions, or hedge funds targeting underperforming companies to push for changes.

Like telling a bad lemonade stand how to do better.
Activist
Types of changes activists push for

Changes in assets, capital structure, cash usage, and governance.

They sell unneeded stuff, manage debt, give out extra money, and fix leadership.
Activist
What are their success rates?

Only 29% of activists succeed overall. Best outcomes from restructuring, sales, or more disclosure.

They’re more likely to win by changing how the shop works, not by firing the boss.
Contrarian
What does a contrarian investor do?

Buys "loser" stocks, expecting market overreaction to reverse in the long term.

Like buying an ignored toy hoping it becomes cool again.
Contrarian
Evidence from DeBondt & Thaler (1987)

Loser portfolios outperformed winner portfolios over 60 months, suggesting market overreaction.

Bad toys often get popular again if you wait long enough.
Contrarian
What does a contrarian investor need?

Self-confidence, patient investors, resilience to losses, and low transaction costs.

You have to believe in your choice even if others laugh at you.
Passive Screener
What is a passive screener?

Someone who filters for undervalued companies using ratios like PBV, PE, and dividend yield.

It’s like using a treasure map to find cheap toys with hidden value.
Passive Screener
What are Graham's value screens?

Low Price-to-Book (PBV), low Price-to-Earnings (PE), high Dividend Yield—backed by long-term evidence.

Buy stuff that looks cheap when compared to what it earns or owns.
Passive Screener
Why is this approach harder today?

Markets are more efficient and more investors are using similar tools, reducing the number of bargains.

It’s like more kids hunting for the same toys—you have to be really smart or really fast.
