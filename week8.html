What is a value investor?
Someone who buys a stock if it’s cheaper than the value of its current assets. Like buying a shop worth £70 for £50.
What are the 3 types of value investors?
Passive Screener, Contrarian, and Activist investors. Each finds undervalued stocks differently.
What does a passive screener do?
Uses filters like low PE or PBV to find cheap stocks. Like using a checklist to buy toys on sale.
What does a contrarian investor believe?
Markets overreact. They buy “loser” stocks expecting a rebound—like picking a toy no one wants, hoping it gets cool again.
Who are activist investors?
They invest in underperforming companies and try to change how they’re run to boost value—like fixing a messy lemonade stand.
How do activist investors create value?
By changing asset use, capital structure, cash policies, or management/governance.
Are activist strategies always successful?
No. About 29% succeed. They do better with restructuring or selling the firm than replacing CEOs or demanding dividends.
How do markets react to activist investors?
Markets usually react positively, especially before the activist announcement is made public.
What does evidence say about contrarian investing?
Loser stocks often outperform winners over long periods, but it takes time and patience to work.
What traits does a contrarian investor need?
Confidence, patience, resilience to bad performance, and awareness of costs like transaction fees.
Why are Graham's screens useful?
Low PE, low PBV, and high dividend yields can help find undervalued stocks—but understanding context is key.
Can investors follow Buffett's method easily?
Not easily—today’s markets are more crowded and efficient. Buffett also uses activism and patience.
