Week 8 – Value Investing Flashcards

What is value investing?
Buying companies whose assets are worth more than their market price. Future growth is a bonus.
3 types of value investors?
Passive Screener, Contrarian Investor, Activist Investor
Who are activist investors?
Individuals, institutions, or hedge funds that push underperforming firms to change—especially in governance.
What changes do activists make?
Asset sales, capital structure changes, cash usage (dividends/buybacks), governance fixes.
When are activist requests more successful?
Taking private, selling the firm, or restructuring—not dividend boosts or firing CEOs.
How does the market react to activism?
Positive reaction—half of 5% excess return happens before public announcement, with increased trading volume.
What’s the long-term impact of activism?
No evidence of harm. ROA and firm performance usually improve over 5 years.
Reactive vs. Proactive activist strategies?
Reactive = invest after action. Proactive = predict targets early (based on cash, scandals, low returns).
Graham & Dodd passive screener?
Screen for low P/B, low P/E, high yield stocks. Also followed by Buffett & Munger.
What do contrarian investors believe?
Markets overreact. They buy underperforming stocks expecting mean reversion. "Losers" often outperform.
Risks of contrarian investing?
Requires confidence, patience, and low costs. Long holding periods needed to succeed.
